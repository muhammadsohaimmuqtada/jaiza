Where most companies stand today.

AI adoption in most companies happens in one of two ways: either nobody has a plan, and employees pick their own tools with no policy behind it — or the company bought an enterprise plan without a clear picture of who actually needs it, for what, leaving expensive seats unused and no measurable return.

Both paths create the same two outcomes: money spent without a clear benefit, and company or client data handled by tools nobody properly reviewed.

01 / SHADOW AI

Employees using personal AI accounts for work, with no policy or visibility into what's actually happening.

02 / DATA EXPOSURE

Company or client data processed by tools with no enterprise agreement or clear retention terms — including cases where most of a team has approved access, but one or two employees don't, and route sensitive data through personal accounts instead.

03 / UNCLEAR ROI

AI spend that isn't tied to any measurable benefit — sometimes costing more than the value it returns.

04 / NO GOVERNANCE PLAN

No process for reviewing, updating, or enforcing AI policy as the team and tool landscape change.

OUR APPROACH

A complete path from adoption to governance.

01

Assessment

We map your company's size, team structure, and actual workflows, and identify every AI tool already in use, sanctioned or not — through interviews, expense records, and workflow review.

02

Custom Adoption Plan

Based on your team's real needs, we recommend the specific tools and tiers that fit — down to the role level. Where relevant, we also assess whether a locally hosted model, a rented GPU server, or a standard API-based plan is the better fit for your budget, data sensitivity, and technical capacity — vendor-neutral, based on what actually suits you rather than a fixed recommendation.

03

ROI & Utilization Review

For companies already on a paid or enterprise plan: we review whether your current AI spend matches actual usage and value. If you're paying for enterprise seats that sit idle, or a plan that costs more than the value it returns, we'll tell you plainly — including recommending you scale down, not just up.

04

Governance & Data Protection

We build a governance policy your team can realistically follow, and check that it's actually enforced — including catching cases where some employees have proper access but others are bypassing it entirely, whether by habit or oversight.

05

Ongoing Review

AI usage and tooling change constantly. We offer periodic re-checks to confirm your policy still holds, your tooling still fits, and no new exposure has appeared since your last review.

THE CERTIFICATE

Documentation, when you need it.

Pakistan does not yet have a comprehensive, enacted data protection law — the Personal Data Protection Bill remains in draft, and the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016, amended in 2025, addresses data misuse as a criminal matter rather than a full privacy framework. That means most Pakistani companies are not legally required, today, to prove how they handle AI and data.

Foreign clients are a different story. A growing number of businesses in the US, UK, and Gulf region require vendors to formally attest to how client data is handled — including whether it passes through AI tools, and under what terms. If your clients (or their regulators) require this kind of attestation, we provide a formal certificate of review documenting your audit and current governance status.

If your business doesn't yet face that requirement, we still recommend building good data-handling habits now — both because Pakistani law is expected to catch up eventually, and because it protects you regardless of what any regulator requires.

TIMING

Why now.

Foreign enterprises are increasingly updating vendor security questionnaires to include AI data-handling requirements, and a growing number explicitly ask how client data interacts with external language models. At the same time, AI tool costs are rising, and companies that adopted quickly without a plan are starting to ask whether the spend is actually paying off.

Both problems point to the same fix: a clear, honest picture of what you're using, what it's costing you, and what it's exposing — and a plan to fix all three.
